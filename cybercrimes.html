Understanding Cyber Crimes

Cybercrimes are unlawful activities carried out using computers, digital devices, networks, or the internet. In some cases, technology is the target of the crime; in others, it is used as a tool to commit traditional offenses. Cybercrime can involve stealing personal information, conducting online scams, disrupting computer systems, impersonating individuals, or accessing confidential data without authorization. As digital technology continues to evolve, recognizing these offenses has become increasingly important.

Types of Cyber Crime

Phishing

Phishing is a form of online deception in which criminals impersonate trusted organizations or individuals to obtain sensitive information. These attacks commonly involve fraudulent emails, text messages, or websites designed to appear legitimate.

Example : A fraudulent message pretending to come from a bank requests account verification through a suspicious link.

Identity Theft

Identity theft occurs when someone obtains and misuses another person's personal information without permission. Stolen information may be used for impersonation, fraudulent transactions, or unauthorized account access.

Example : A criminal uses another person's personal details to impersonate them online.

Ransomware

Ransomware is malicious software that encrypts files or restricts access to computer systems. Attackers demand payment in exchange for a promised method of restoring access, although payment does not guarantee recovery.

Example: A company becomes unable to access important business files following a ransomware attack.

Online Financial Fraud

Online financial fraud involves deception intended to steal money or obtain financial information. Common forms include fake online stores, fraudulent investment offers, payment scams, and impersonation schemes.

Example: A fake shopping website accepts payment for a product that is never delivered.

Cyberbullying and Online Harassment

Cyberbullying and online harassment involve using digital platforms to repeatedly intimidate, threaten, humiliate, or target another person. Such behavior can negatively affect a person's emotional well-being, privacy, and sense of security.

Example: An individual repeatedly targets another person with abusive messages or humiliating posts.

Data Theft and Data Breaches

Data theft involves obtaining information without authorization, while a data breach occurs when sensitive information is exposed or accessed improperly. These incidents can compromise personal records, business information, and confidential organizational data.

Example: Unauthorized access to a company's database exposes customer information.

Consequences of Cyber Crime

Cybercrime can produce consequences that extend far beyond the devices or accounts directly affected.

  • Financial Damage - Victims may lose money, face fraudulent transactions, or incur expenses while recovering their accounts and systems. away sensitive information.
  • Privacy Violations - Personal, financial, or confidential information may be exposed, misused, or distributed without permission.
  • Emotional Distress - Personal, financial, or confidential information may be exposed, misused, or distributed without permission.
  • Organization disruption - Businesses and institutions may experience operational delays, reputational damage, and costly recovery processes.
  • Loss of trust - Cybercrime can weaken public confidence in digital platforms, online services, and electronic transactions.

What to do if you become a victim?

  • Secure affected accounts and change compromised passwords from a trusted device.
  • Enable multifactor authentication.
  • Contact your bank immediately if financial information or payments are involved.
  • Save relevant evidence, such as messages and transaction records.
  • Finish with a prominent button: Know Where to Report
